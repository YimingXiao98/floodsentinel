# Post hoc: FloodSentinel flood status against the NCEI Storm Events Database

Post hoc, not prespecified. Generated by `eval/posthoc_storm_events.py` from frozen artifacts and the Storm Events bulk CSVs listed below; the frozen directories were only read.

## What this is and is not

The frozen studies grade policies against a hydrologic reference built from the same USGS/NWPS gauge series and flood-stage thresholds the policies read, so that reference cannot confirm that a reported flood corresponds to flooding recorded by anyone else. This comparison uses an external record neither the policies nor the reference see: county-level Flood and Flash Flood entries in the NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database. Storm Events is a report-based record compiled by NWS forecast offices from spotter, media, emergency-management, and official reports. Its begin and end times are often approximate or rounded, its coverage depends on who reported what, it records impacts (roads closed, water in structures) rather than stage above a threshold, and a single physical flood may appear as several entries or none. Agreement and disagreement below therefore describe consistency between two imperfect records; this is a comparison with an independent record, not a validation against ground truth.

Main comparison: EVENT_TYPE in {Flood, Flash Flood}, CZ_TYPE C, and STATE_FIPS plus CZ_FIPS equal to the county FIPS. Coastal Flood and Heavy Rain county-type entries are counted per county-event but not used. Coastal Flood is normally filed against forecast zones (CZ_TYPE Z), whose numbers are not county codes and cannot be matched to a county without a zone-county correlation table, so zone rows are ignored and the Coastal Flood counts here are close to zero by construction; they do not measure coastal flooding in these counties. A Storm Events row is kept when it overlaps the analysis window [analysis_start, analysis_end).

Puerto Rico: Storm Events files Puerto Rico under STATE_FIPS 99, not the Census code 72, and records municipios as county-type rows (CZ_TYPE C) whose CZ_FIPS is the Census municipio code (Caguas 25, Ponce 113). The script therefore maps state 72 to 99 and matches CZ_FIPS as usual; the matched CZ_NAME values are listed per county-event and were checked against the county name.

Time zones: BEGIN_DATE_TIME and END_DATE_TIME are local times in the zone named by CZ_TIMEZONE; the signed number in the label (CST-6, EST-5, AST-4) is the UTC offset in hours, so UTC = local minus offset. Every flood-related row in all nine files, including July and August rows, carries a standard-time label (no EDT or CDT labels appear), consistent with the Storm Data convention of local standard time, and the offset was applied as stated with no daylight-saving adjustment. The local date-time strings were cross-checked against BEGIN/END_YEARMONTH, _DAY, and _TIME on every matched row: 2790 fields checked, 0 disagreements. Examples of the conversion are in summary.json (`storm_parse_checks.examples`).

Matching: a reference episode [s, e) and a Storm Events entry [b, f] match at tolerance T when b minus T is at or before e and s is at or before f plus T. An acquisition at instant t falls within an entry when b minus T is at or before t and t is at or before f plus T. The primary tolerance is 6 h; 0 h and 24 h are reported as sensitivity. Reference status during a Storm Events entry is the reference label (1 h maximum reference age, three-valued) on the 15-minute study grid within the entry plus or minus 6 h, clipped to the analysis window: positive if any grid point is positive, negative if all are known negative, unknown if all are unknown, negative_and_unknown otherwise.

Replays: acquisition instants and per-acquisition verdicts are not stored in the frozen artifacts, so the hybrid and phase adaptation were replayed for every county-event through `eval.run_replay.run` under the frozen replay environment with the helpers of `paper/figures/make_mechanism_figure.py`. Every replay had to reproduce the frozen acquisition count, termination, fold instant, acquisitions by reason, burst spans, immediate fires, and trigger consultations, and the stored reference episodes had to equal the episodes recomputed from the corpus, or the script stops. Two per-acquisition flags are reported: `flood_stage_observed` (some gauge in the acquisition at minor flood stage or above; the flag the frozen episode grading uses) and `is_flooding` (the county regional verdict, true at action stage or above; the operational flag behind the reported status).

Limitations of this comparison: (1) Storm Events files are indexed by begin year and every analysis window lies within one calendar year, so an entry that began in an earlier year and ran into a window would be missed; no window starts before 6 March, so none is expected. (2) Only county-type rows are matched; zone-filed flood impacts (tropical cyclone, surge, coastal) are listed as context for county-events without county entries but not matched. (3) A mission that folds stops acquiring, so Storm Events entries after the fold cannot be reported by that policy; they are counted and flagged. (4) The plus or minus 6 h tolerance is a choice; 0 h and 24 h are shown and the conclusions should be read across all three. (5) Storm Events entries for one storm are often many near-duplicate rows (for example several Flash Flood rows for different locations in the same county within an hour), so entry counts are not independent floods.
